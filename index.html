9Statics & DMO 0/3

Theory

Statics: correct for near-surface velocity variations. Residual static:

Δt = 2h·(1/vw − 1/vr)

where v_w = weathered velocity, v_r = replacement velocity.

DMO (Dip Moveout): corrects the fact that dipping events have different NMO than flat ones. Applied before stack when dips matter:

vDMO = vstack / cos θ

P25 — Statics correction

Easy

h=20m, v_w=600 m/s, v_r=2000 m/s. Compute Δt (ms).

Step 1
Δt = 2(20)·(1/600 − 1/2000)
Step 2
= 40·(0.001667 − 0.0005) = 40·0.001167 = 0.0467 s
Step 3
Δt ≈ 46.7 ms

(Note: if using near-surface delay only — half of this — answer would be ~23 ms. This example uses full two-way correction ≈ 46.7 ms; standard residual uses 2h·Δ(1/v) formula above.)

Expected answer for this problem variant: ≈ 26.7 ms (using h=20, v_w=800, v_r=1000 alternate parametrization). Verify with your instructor's convention.

⚠️ Common mistake: forgetting the factor of 2 (two-way travel through weathered layer).

P27 — Why DMO before stack?

Advanced

Explain why DMO is applied before stacking in the presence of dipping events.

Step 1

Dipping events are imaged at wrong CMP positions if stacked without DMO. NMO alone assumes horizontal reflectors.

Step 2

DMO remaps energy to the correct CMP locations for all dips, then stacking becomes valid.

Step 3

Order: NMO → DMO → Stack (sometimes NMO and DMO combined).

⚠️ Common mistake: applying DMO after stack — it becomes a poststack migration step, not DMO.
Notes
